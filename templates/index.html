    The idea

    Everyone types their password with their own rhythm. Some people hold keys longer, some roll quickly through letters they type a lot, some pause before the numbers. That rhythm is surprisingly hard to copy, even if you know the password.

    What gets measured

    For every key: how long it's held down. Between every pair of keys: the time from one press to the next, and from one release to the next press. A 10 character password gives 28 numbers per attempt.

    Signing up

    You type the password 8 times. The app works out your average for each of those numbers and how much it usually varies. If one sample is way off (you got distracted, a typo you pushed through) it's thrown out.

    Logging in

    Each number from the new attempt is compared to your average, scaled by how much that number normally varies for you. Those are averaged into one distance score. Below the threshold you're in. This is called scaled Manhattan distance and it's one of the best simple detectors in keystroke dynamics research.

    Speed check

    The app also remembers how long you usually take to type the whole password. Someone who copies your pattern but types it a lot faster or slower than you ever do gets blocked, even if their rhythm score looks fine.

    Getting better over time

    Every successful login gets added to your profile (up to the last 15), so it keeps up as your typing changes.

    Limits

    It needs a physical keyboard. Phones don't report key timings properly. It's meant as an extra check on top of the password, not a replacement for it.
