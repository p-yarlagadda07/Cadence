      The idea

      Everyone types their password with their own rhythm. Some people hold keys longer, some roll quickly through letters they type a lot, some pause before the numbers. That rhythm is surprisingly hard to copy, even if you know the password.

      What gets measured

      For every key: how long it's held down. Between every pair of keys: the time from one press to the next. A 10 character password gives 19 numbers per attempt.

      The Shift key

      For capitals and symbols, it also measures how early you press Shift before the letter, how long you keep holding it after, and whether you use the left or right Shift. People are surprisingly consistent about this, and it's very different from person to person.

      Signing up

      You type the password 8 times. The app works out your average for each of those numbers and how much it usually varies. If one sample is way off (you got distracted, a typo you pushed through) it's thrown out.

      Logging in

      Each number from the new attempt is compared to your average, scaled by how much that number normally varies for you. Those are averaged into one distance score. Below the threshold you're in. The two numbers furthest from your usual are left out, so one hesitation doesn't sink an attempt. This is called scaled Manhattan distance and it's one of the best simple detectors in keystroke dynamics research.

      What a failed login shows

      Nothing beyond "Login failed". Showing the score or which check failed would tell someone how close they got, and they could keep adjusting until they got in. Your charts only appear after you've logged in.

      Security alerts

      If someone types your correct password but their typing doesn't match, the attempt is saved. Next time you log in, you see what happened and why it was blocked, for example that they held a key twice as long as you or used the other Shift key. A blocked attempt with the right password means someone knows it, so it's a good moment to change it. Only key positions are stored, never the characters, so the alert doesn't leak your password.

      Speed check

      The app also remembers how long you usually take to type the whole password. Someone who copies your pattern but types it a lot faster or slower than you ever do gets blocked, even if their rhythm score looks fine.

      Lockout

      After {{ max_fails }} failed logins in a row, whether it's a wrong password or the wrong rhythm, the account locks for 15 minutes. That stops someone from just retrying until they get lucky. You get a recovery code when you sign up, and it unlocks the account straight away.

      Testing by other people

      The owner can open their account for testing for a set time. Anyone visiting can then try to log in as them, but only sees "Let in" or "Blocked", with {{ public_tries }} tries each. That's enough to measure how well it keeps people out without turning into a practice tool.

      Getting better over time

      Every successful login gets added to your profile (up to the last 15), so it keeps up as your typing changes.

      Limits

      It needs a physical keyboard. Phones don't report key timings properly. It's meant as an extra check on top of the password, not a replacement for it.
